2. The 7 C’s of Effective Communication

Click a card to reveal its exact definition and an example violation in Banking:

1. Clear

Direct & Simple

Click to Flip
Definition: Message must be direct and easy to understand without confusion. Bad Example: "We might process your loan depending on various ambient parameters."
2. Concise

Brief & To the Point

Click to Flip
Definition: Keep it short; eliminate unnecessary filler words. Bad Example: "In my personal opinion at this moment in time, I think you should perhaps fill out this form."
3. Concrete

Specific Facts & Figures

Click to Flip
Definition: Use specific facts, exact numbers, and explicit details. Bad Example: "Interest rate is pretty low right now." (Better: "Interest rate is 6.5% p.a.")
4. Correct

Error-Free Language

Click to Flip
Definition: Free from grammatical mistakes, spelling errors, and wrong facts. Bad Example: Incorrect bank account numbers or spelling account holder names wrong.
5. Coherent

Logical Flow

Click to Flip
Definition: Logic flows naturally and sentences connect smoothly. Bad Example: Jumping from loan interest rates directly to bank branch closing time without transition.
6. Complete

All Necessary Info

Click to Flip
Definition: Contains all details required so receiver doesn't have to guess. Bad Example: "Meeting scheduled tomorrow." (Missing: What time? Which branch? What room?)
7. Courteous

Polite & Respectful

Click to Flip
Definition: Polite, respectful, enthusiastic, and considerate of the listener. Bad Example: Using rude tones or demanding words like "Give me your ID immediately!"

MINTS Rule for Capitalization

  • M - Months & Days January, Monday
  • I - The pronoun "I" "I will apply for a loan."
  • N - Names of people/places Rahul, Delhi, State Bank
  • T - Titles Dr., Mr., Manager Roy
  • S - Starting letter First letter of every sentence

Active vs. Passive Voice Transformer

Active Voice:

"The manager approved the home loan."

Subject (Manager) performs the action.
Passive Voice:

"The home loan was approved by the manager."

Subject receives the action.

3. Time Management Steps

1. Organize

Plan tasks daily using a planner, to-do list, or digital schedule.

2. Prioritize

Focus on urgent and important tasks first (e.g. Business Math before casual reading).

3. Control

Avoid procrastination, limit social media notifications, and set firm deadlines.

4. Track

Evaluate time spent on each task and optimize for the next day.

2. Cyber Threat Identification Matrix

High Exam Weightage

Learn the precise distinctions between malware types to avoid losing marks in short-answer questions:

VIRUS

Attaches to existing files & requires human action to spread.

Example: Opening an infected `.exe` email attachment that corrupts banking software.

WORM

Standalone malware that replicates automatically across networks.

Example: Spreads independently from PC to PC across bank server networks without human intervention.

TROJAN HORSE

Disguised as legitimate useful software to create a back door.

Example: A fake bank calculator app that secretly allows hackers access to confidential files.

PHISHING

Fraudulent attempt to steal confidential login/card info via fake emails.

Example: Fake bank email asking you to click a link to "verify account password urgently".

SPYWARE

Secretly records keystrokes and tracks user online activity.

Example: Keyloggers capturing net-banking passwords entered on public computers.

5 BEST SECURITY PRACTICES
  • • 8-12 char strong password with symbols
  • • Keep updated Antivirus definitions
  • • Enable Firewall to block bad traffic
  • • Verify **HTTPS** in browser URL
  • • Take regular external data backups
